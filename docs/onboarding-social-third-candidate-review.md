## Remaining failing cases

### C05: efficient but uniformly generic

After "Deadlines," the assistant says:

> "Deadlines leave little room for surprises, especially when you're preparing annual accounts. When you're ready, create your account and explore the SORP guidance for your work with Interim Spaces."

This is relevant and polite, but it remains a general observation applicable to almost any accountant. The preceding conversation also largely paraphrases role and referral fields. There is insufficient evidence of perceptive listening across the journey to meet the demanding threshold.

The failure does not establish a rule that every short answer needs a follow-up. The curiosity rating is 3, not a penalty for refusing to ask a mandatory question. Rather, the whole conversation remains mostly competent at a 3/4 level, with efficiency its principal strength. A brief genuine concern should remain an opportunity for meaningful engagement; brevity alone does not establish reluctance.

### C09: acknowledgement becomes an unsupported financial assessment

The user says the budget shows four months of unrestricted reserves. The assistant responds:

> "Four months of unrestricted reserves leaves limited room to manoeuvre, so your concern about winter is understandable."

The user's worry is established. The adequacy of four months' reserves is not. Cash flow, commitments, income timing, risk exposure and the charity's reserve policy are not supplied. The assistant moves from receiving the worry to making its own evaluative financial claim from an isolated figure.

This fails the required social-judgement minimum at 2/4. The claim is not classified as proven false or as a categorical original hard-gate violation; the problem is unwarranted confidence. Acknowledging the person's concern should not quietly add a professional assessment that the conversation cannot support.

### C22: the factual distinction is clearer, but the repair remains defensive

After "I already told you Interim Spaces," the response is:

> "You did — Interim Spaces. I was checking whether you wanted to start with that organisation, not asking for its name again. We'll still need to check its official charity record. Shall we start there?"

The reply now explains a real distinction, which is an improvement over the preceding candidate. However, "I was checking ... not asking" centres the assistant's defence of its intent. It can sound like a correction of the irritated user. The final permission question then revisits substantially the same decision.

The repair needs to preserve the selection boundary while accepting that the prior wording did not land clearly and making the next step easy to understand. This is a general conversational requirement, not a prescribed sentence. Acknowledgement scores 2/4. The later generic deadline ending does not offset the repair weakness.

This is not labelled a strict repeated-fact hard gate: the user's first selection confirmation was still unresolved. The failure is how that legitimate boundary is communicated.

## General defects still worth addressing

1. **Do not turn an isolated fact into an unsupported judgement.** C09 is the consequential example. Receiving a worry, recognising its seriousness and assessing financial adequacy are different acts.
2. **Repair the experience rather than defend intent.** C22's information is more accurate, but its social stance still feels corrective toward the user.
3. **Keep short concerns distinct from refusals and uncertainty.** The deadline endings in C05/C12/C22 are efficient but generic. A productive question is optional; meaningful engagement must remain possible.
4. **Avoid invented contrasts.** C06 twice contrasts evidence with whether disclosures "read well," although the user never raised presentation quality. The substantive evidence question is useful; the extra contrast adds an ungrounded issue and makes the ending sound rehearsed.
5. **Avoid overstating emotional recognition on every turn.** C25 repeatedly describes the pressure and uncertainty of leading a charity facing closure. Recognition is warranted, but repeating it after each answer can become burdensome.
6. **Keep the stated purpose of the broad question coherent.** In C17 the assistant retrospectively calls it an oddly personal way to ask about reporting concerns. Elsewhere it presents the question as genuinely broad. The response to an objection should not require inventing a narrower prior purpose.
